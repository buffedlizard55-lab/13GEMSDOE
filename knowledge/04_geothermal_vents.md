# Hidden geothermal vents — verified research base

**Purpose:** preserve source-linked background relevant to geothermal systems and the competition's fault-mapping task. The competition target is fault geometry, not a verified geothermal-vent inventory. Source statements in §§1–3 are literature or organizer claims; §4 contains hypotheses and must not be read as established competition signal or private-test evidence.

**Verification date: 2026-09-30.** Links were fetched this session or previously verified in `knowledge/01_verified_facts.md`; a linked source does not verify every interpretation in this file.

## 1. Why faults are the geothermal target

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|------|--------|
| G1 | The competition task is to predict **geological faults** as indicators of **geothermal resources**; labels come from USGS Quaternary Faults and new expert-labelled faults at NLR/USGS. | [Problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) · [Official Rules §2](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf) |
| G2 | INGENIOUS goal is to *\"accelerate discoveries of new, commercially viable **hidden** geothermal systems\"* — hidden = no surface expression. | [GDR 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) (INGENIOUS landing page) |
| G3 | Historical hydrothermal estimates relied on temperature/heat flow alone and **ignored permeability and fluids**; more accurate hidden hydrothermal estimates require permeability and fluids proxies. | [Trainor-Guitton et al. 2025](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3018341) – abstract |
| G4 | INGENIOUS provides **48 geologic/geophysical feature layers at 250 m** as permeability proxies (earthquake rates, shear stress/strain, conductivity) plus fluid proxies; used with 37 operating plant nameplates as labels in XGBoost → reV. | [Trainor-Guitton et al. 2025](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3018341) – §2.2 |
| G5 | The reV geothermal module input must be **MW potential**, not favourability; favourability cannot be used in TEA calculations. | [Trainor-Guitton et al. 2025](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3018341) – Introduction |

## 2. Where vents actually sit (structural settings)

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|------|--------|
| S1 | Great Basin geothermal systems catalogued into **8 major groups**: major normal fault segments, **fault intersections**, **accommodation zones** (belts of intermeshing oppositely-dipping normal faults), **displacement transfer zones** (strike-slip terminating in normal arrays), etc. | [Faulds et al. Structural Inventory dataset 1148722](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/1148722) – abstract |
| S2 | *\"Many geothermal systems occupy **discrete steps** in fault zones or lie in **zones of intersecting, overlapping, and/or intermeshing faults**\"* and *\"Geothermal systems are **rare along major range-front faults**, possibly due to both reduced permeability in thick zones of **clay gouge**\"*; step-overs/terminations/intersections/accommodation zones are *\"long-term, critically stressed areas, where fluid pathways would more likely remain open in networks of closely-spaced, **breccia-dominated fractures**.\"* Many higher-enthalpy systems are **hybrids** (e.g., intersection within broad accommodation zone). | [Faulds et al. dataset 1148722](https://www.osti.gov/dataexplorer/biblio/dataset/1148722) – abstract |
| S3 | BRIDGE confirmation: *\"**Overlapping, oppositely dipping normal fault systems generate multiple fault intersections** in the upper part of the reservoir\"* which provide *\"convenient **channel ways** for geothermal fluids\"* and *\"highly fractured **subvertical conduits** that accommodate ascent of the hydrothermal fluids.\"* | [BRIDGE Final Report SAND2025-01826](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) |
| S4 | *\"Productive geothermal wells appear to be **localized along the steeply E-dipping Salt Wells fault zone as it loses displacement southward, breaks into several **splays (i.e., horsetails)**, and intermeshes with the W-dipping fault system. The **increased fracture density** generated by the **multiple intersecting faults** produced greater permeability\"* — horse-tailing ends, not main segments. | [BRIDGE report](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) – Salt Wells example |
| S5 | *\"Many fields do **not** reside on the **main segments** of major range-front faults but rather on the **horse-tailing ends** of major faults or on **less conspicuous normal fault zones**. Many geothermal systems occupy **discrete steps** in fault zones or lie in zones of **intersecting, overlapping, and/or accommodation zones**\"* | [BRIDGE report](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) + [Faulds & Hinz GRC 2012 paper](https://publications.mygeoenergynow.org/grc/1030317.pdf) – Astor Pass blind system |
| S6 | Blind Astor Pass system: *\"controlled by the **intersection of northwest-striking dextral and north-northwest-striking normal faults** … Wells drilled into the southwestern quadrant of the **fault intersection** yielded 94 °C fluids … The blind geothermal system is hosted ... **Two north- to northwest-plunging fault intersections**\"* — characterized only by integrating multiple geophysical datasets in 3D. | [Faulds et al. GRC 2012 Astor Pass](https://publications.mygeoenergynow.org/grc/1030317.pdf) |
| S7 | Favourable settings include *\"**step-overs**, **fault intersections**, **accommodation zones**, **horse-tailing fault terminations** and **transtensional pull-aparts**\"* as most effective controls; blind systems have *\"no surface expression\"* so costs mitigate via 3D structural understanding. | [GRC 2012 paper](https://publications.mygeoenergynow.org/grc/1030317.pdf) – abstract/keywords |

## 3. How hidden systems are found (INGENIOUS / BRIDGE methods)

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|------|--------|
| M1 | Nevada Play Fairway Analysis (PFA) combines distributions of **heat, permeability, fluid** factors into favourability; BRIDGE and INGENIOUS build on it with workflows combining conceptual modelling, machine learning, geostatistics, value-of-information. | [BRIDGE report](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) – §1 |
| M2 | BRIDGE prospect table: **PFA structural targets** include *fault intersection*, *displacement transfer zone*, *pull-apart*, *stepover*, *fault termination* — many with *\"**no known thermal anomaly**\"* and *\"**no clear HTEM signature**\"* yet prioritized structurally. | [BRIDGE report prospect table](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) – Phase 2/3 table |
| M3 | Conceptual model elements: *hot upflow*, *cooler outflow*, *low vertical permeability cap*, and mechanisms that **hide** the system (*conductive heat loss, mixing with meteoric water*). Where low-resistivity smectite cap exists, MT/HTEM can see it; where not, **structural** targeting is primary. | [BRIDGE report](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1682/BRIDGE_Final_Report_SAND2025-01826.pdf) – conceptual model |
| M4 | GeoDAWN data (high-res lidar, magnetic, radiometric) is at 100 m in competition but native airborne is higher resolution; USGS ScienceBase item 657e1d85 and DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ document the survey. | [USGS GeoDAWN landing](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5066/P93LGLVQ) |
| M5 | INGENIOUS Great Basin Regional Dataset Compilation **DOI 10.15121/1881483**, CC-BY-4.0, contains Quaternary Faults v2 (with ages/slip rates), Paleo Geothermal Features (sinter/tufa), 2 m probes, Q volcanics, heat flow, MT conductance — all verified to exist at GDR 1391 but downloads **blocked by sandbox egress** (limitation L-2). | [GDR 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) – files list |

## 4. Research implications (hypotheses, not verified competition signal)

1. **Structural settings worth testing.** The cited Great Basin literature describes
   geothermal systems at some fault step-overs, intersections, transfer zones, and
   horse-tailing terminations. This supports testing those settings as geological
   hypotheses; it does not imply that every mapped junction is permeable or that a
   detector-output crossing marks a geothermal system.

2. **R8-5 is not a direct permeability or vent measurement.** Its “intersection
   density” is calculated from crossings among candidate raster ridges. Those ridges
   are not independently confirmed faults. R8-5 is a secondary image transform, and
   its geothermal interpretation remains unvalidated.

3. **Terrain and gravity transforms remain exploratory.** R8-1a/b (openness/TPI),
   R8-2 (flow), and R8-3 (gravity–topography coherence) generate testable features;
   drainage, depositional edges, regional compensation, resolution, and processing
   differences are plausible confounds. The features themselves do not establish that
   faults or vents are present. In the historical five-fold direct-DTI screen at
   cov0.05/sp3, openness was near the topo reference (0.07807 vs 0.07844), while TPI,
   flow, and isocoherence scored 0.06922, 0.07319, and 0.03143. These are local
   catalogue-recovery measurements only. In the later visible-only confirmation,
   the broad R8 union scored below the topo comparator (worst-rule mean DTI 0.05584
   vs 0.08687); no R8 artifact is cleared for submission. See
   [`reports/holdout_candidate_r8_2026-09-30.json`](../reports/holdout_candidate_r8_2026-09-30.json).

4. **Magnetic-product disagreement is only a hypothesis.** R8-4 compares transformed
   magnetic products. A mismatch may reflect magnetization, lithology, or processing;
   it is not by itself evidence of a fault, remanence, permeability, or a vent.

5. **Phase-2 expert review does not validate this detector suite.** The competition
   description and rules describe expert review of submitted predictions as part of
   the expanded label set. That makes a defensible, well-documented map important; it
   does not turn the R8 recipe into a verified vent map or justify an upload. The
   archived R8 raster is retained for review only, with `NOT_CLEARED` status.
